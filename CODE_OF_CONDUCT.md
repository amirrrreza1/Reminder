# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

We are committed to a welcoming, safe, and productive community for people of every background and experience level. Participation is judged by conduct and contribution, not identity, status, language fluency, or familiarity with the project.

## Expected behavior

- Be respectful, specific, and patient.
- Critique ideas and code, not people.
- Assume good intent while addressing concrete impact.
- Accept correction, apologize when appropriate, and improve behavior.
- Keep reviews focused on the project’s documented goals and evidence.
- Respect privacy. Never share another person’s contact details, private reports, credentials, logs, or personal reminder data.
- Make space for new contributors and explain project conventions without gatekeeping.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, discrimination, threats, stalking, or unwanted sexual attention.
- Insults, personal attacks, ridicule, intimidation, or sustained disruption.
- Trolling, deliberate misinformation, or repeatedly derailing technical discussion.
- Publishing private information or correspondence without permission.
- Pressuring anyone to disclose identity, location, employer, credentials, or personal schedules.
- Abusing project infrastructure to spam, send unsolicited notifications, mine data, or test systems without authorization.
- Retaliation against someone who raises a concern in good faith.

## Scope

This policy applies in project repositories, issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, community chats, events, and private project communication. It also applies when someone officially represents the project.

## Reporting

Do not put sensitive conduct details in a public issue. If a private maintainer contact is published in the repository/community profile, use it. If no private route is available, open a content-free issue titled `Private moderation contact requested`; include no names, screenshots, or allegations. A maintainer who is not involved will arrange a private channel.

For threats, doxxing, or abuse hosted by GitHub, also use GitHub’s [reporting tools](https://support.github.com/contact/report-abuse). Security vulnerabilities follow [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md), not this process.

Reports should include, when safe:

- what happened and where;
- links/timestamps or preserved evidence;
- immediate safety or privacy concerns;
- whether anyone involved has a conflict of interest;
- the outcome requested, if any.

Do not investigate other people or solicit more private information. Preserve only what is needed for the report.

## Enforcement

Maintainers will prioritize safety and privacy, limit report access, hear relevant perspectives, and avoid conflicts of interest. Possible actions include:

1. private clarification or correction;
2. formal warning with required behavior change;
3. temporary participation limits;
4. removal of content or privileges;
5. permanent ban and escalation to the hosting platform when necessary.

Actions depend on impact, pattern, intent, response to correction, and community safety. Knowingly false or retaliatory reports may themselves violate this policy; an unsubstantiated good-faith report does not.

## Privacy and transparency

Report details are shared only with people needed to evaluate and respond. Maintainers may publish anonymized aggregate moderation notes when useful, but will not identify reporters or affected people without permission unless required for immediate safety or law.

## Appeals

A person subject to a moderation action may request one appeal through the private contact used for the decision. Someone not involved in the original decision should review it when possible. The appeal should identify a factual/process error or meaningful new information; disagreement alone does not require reversal.

## Maintainer readiness

Before accepting broad external contributions, the repository owner should publish a durable private moderation contact and identify at least two people, when possible, who can handle conflicts of interest. Until then, public participation should remain limited enough that reports can be handled responsibly.
